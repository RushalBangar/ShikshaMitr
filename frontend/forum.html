How do I determine whether roots of a quadratic equation are real, equal, or imaginary without full factoring?

In ax² + bx + c = 0, I get confused when the discriminant D = b² − 4ac is zero versus negative in state board sample papers. Can someone explain step-by-step with an example?

AK Aman K. • Class 10B (St. Xavier's) ⭐ 420 Karma
👨‍🏫 Verified Faculty Answer by Dr. R. Sharma Senior Math Faculty Verified 1 hr ago
Remember the three discriminant rules:
  • D > 0: Roots are real and distinct.
  • D = 0: Roots are real and equal (x = −b / 2a).
  • D < 0: No real roots (complex).
Example: For x² − 6x + 9 = 0: a=1, b=−6, c=9
D = (−6)² − 4(1)(9) = 36 − 36 = 0 ⇒ Twin roots: x = −(−6)/(2×1) = 3.

Why does electric resistance increase when resistors are connected in series, but decrease in parallel?

We did the lab experiment with Ohm's law today. Intuitively, adding more components should increase resistance, but our teacher said the total resistance is lower than the smallest resistor in parallel. What is the best physical analogy?

PS Priya S. • Class 10A 🥈 390 Karma
👨‍🏫 Verified Faculty Answer by Prof. Verma
Think of highway toll booths: In series, cars pass through consecutive booths one after another (effective length increases: Req = R₁ + R₂). In parallel, new lanes open side-by-side (effective cross-sectional area increases: 1/Req = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂), allowing more total charges to flow per second.
